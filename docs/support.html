Quick Start

Step 1: Import the visual into your Power BI report from AppSource.

Step 2: Drag a numeric measure into the Values field well. Optionally add a dimension to Detail (rows) for row-level data and drilldown support.

Step 3: Use the Format pane to configure bins, colors, and statistics overlays.

Free vs Pro

The Free tier is the histogram itself: ten bins, mean and median lines, quartiles and IQR shading, the benchmark line, the legend, IBCS mode, axis controls, cross-filtering, keyboard navigation and high contrast — over your whole dataset, with no row limit of ours and no watermark. The Pro tier (via AppSource subscription) adds the analysis:

  • Bin count anywhere from 2 to 100
  • Outlier trimming (lower/upper %)
  • Normal distribution curve overlay
  • Full statistics panel (n, μ, M, σ, min, max)
  • Value labels on bars
  • Custom bar color, opacity, border, and gap

Common Questions

Q: Why does the x-axis stop before my largest value?
A: So that you can see your data. With a long-tailed distribution — amounts, response times, consumption — a single extreme value stretches the axis and pushes every other row into the first bar. The axis therefore focuses on where the data actually lives, using Tukey's fences (Q1 − 1.5·IQR to Q3 + 1.5·IQR), and only when that changes anything. Every row is still counted: values beyond the axis go into the end bar, and a note says so, so the totals, the mean and the standard deviation are exact. With Pro you can set the range yourself through Exclude top / bottom %.

Q: How much can one click cross-filter?
A: Clicking a bar filters the report by every row behind it, up to 10,000 distinct values in a single click — measured as the practical ceiling for a Power BI filter over a live connection to a semantic model. Most bars are far below that. When a bar holds more, the visual says so instead of filtering part of it: filtering a subset would leave the rest of the report showing figures that do not match the bar you clicked, and nothing on screen would reveal it. Raising the bin count (Pro) puts fewer rows in each bar.

Q: How large a dataset can it draw?
A: The whole of it. Power BI hands a visual 30,000 rows at a time; Histogram Pro requests the following segments and combines them, so the distribution is computed over every row — verified at 500,000, and the ceiling from there is Power BI's own. The one exception is Power BI Desktop, which runs inside Electron and cannot stream segments: there it draws the first 30,000 and says so on the chart. Publish the report to the Service for the full distribution.

Q: The visual shows "Add a numeric field to get started"
A: Drag a numeric measure or column into the Values field well on the left panel.

Q: The bins setting doesn't change anything
A: Bin count is fixed at 10 in the Free tier. When you change it without a licence, Power BI shows its own notification with the link to obtain one — the setting is kept and applies as soon as the licence is active.

Q: The normal curve is a Pro feature
A: Every setting that needs a licence says (Pro) in the format pane, and it is never hidden: you can see what the paid tier offers before deciding. Turning one on without a licence leaves the free result on screen and raises Power BI's own notification, with the link to obtain one.

Q: How do drilldown and cross-filtering work?
A: Add a category field to Detail (rows) to enable drilldown. Use the drill-up/drill-down buttons in the visual header. Cross-filtering with other visuals works automatically — clicking a bar highlights the corresponding data across the report.

Q: How do I get a Pro license?
A: Search for "Histogram Pro" in Microsoft AppSource and subscribe to the Pro plan.
