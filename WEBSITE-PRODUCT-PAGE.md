## TAB 1: OVERVIEW

### The shape of your data, not its total

Every other visual on the page tells you how much. A histogram tells you how it is spread
— where the mass sits, how far the tail runs, whether there are two populations hiding
inside what looks like one.

**The problem it solves**
An average is a single number standing in for thousands of rows, and it hides everything
that matters: the long tail, the second peak, the cluster of near-zeros. Power BI has no
native histogram, so the usual answer is a calculated column of bins in DAX — which fixes
the bin count at design time, has to be rewritten to change it, and gives you no mean, no
median and no quartiles.

**How it works**
Drop a numeric measure into Values and the row-level field into Detail (rows). That is the
whole setup. Mean and median are drawn by default; quartiles, IQR shading, a benchmark
line and a normal curve are one toggle each.

**Who it's for**
Anyone who has to answer "is this normal?" — pricing and discount analysis, response and
resolution times, basket sizes, consumption, quality control, credit exposure.

**At a glance**
- The distribution over your whole table, not a sample — verified at 500,000 rows
- Mean, median, quartiles and IQR without a line of DAX
- An axis that focuses on where the data lives instead of chasing one extreme value
- One click filters the report by every row behind the bar
- IBCS monochrome mode
- No network calls of any kind

## TAB 2: FEATURES

Everything below is free except the items marked **(Pro)**.

### The distribution
- A numeric measure binned and drawn, with axis titles, grid and font size under your control
- **Your whole table.** Power BI hands a visual 30,000 rows at a time; Histogram Pro asks
  for the following segments and combines them. Verified at 500,000 rows.
- **An axis built for skewed data.** Amounts, response times and consumption have long
  tails: with the axis running from minimum to maximum, one extreme value pushes every
  other row into the first bar. The axis focuses on where the data lives instead — and
  every row is still counted, with values beyond the axis going into the end bar.
- **(Pro)** Bin count anywhere from 2 to 100
- **(Pro)** Outlier trimming, lower and upper percentage, when you want to set the range
  yourself

### The reference lines
- Mean and median, each with its own colour
- P25, P75 and IQR shading
- A benchmark line for a target or SLA, with its own value, colour and label
- A legend, at the side or along the bottom, with editable labels
- **(Pro)** Normal curve overlay, to check the distribution against a normal of the same
  mean and deviation
- **(Pro)** Statistics panel on the chart: n, mean, median, standard deviation, min and max

### Presentation
- IBCS monochrome mode for standards-based reporting
- **(Pro)** Bar colour, opacity, border colour and width, and the gap between bars
- **(Pro)** Value labels above the bars, with size, colour and an optional percentage

### In the report
- **Cross-filtering by the bar** — one click filters by every row behind it, up to 10,000
  distinct values at once
- Cross-highlighting from other visuals
- Drill-up and drill-down when Detail (rows) holds a hierarchy
- Report page tooltips as well as standard tooltips
- Right-click context menu on a bar and on empty space

### Accessibility
- Keyboard focus and activation on every bar, with descriptive ARIA labels
- High-contrast themes follow the Power BI palette

## TAB 3: TECHNICAL

**Specs**
- Platform: Power BI Desktop, Service and mobile
- Interface: English

**Field wells**
- **Values** (measure, required) — the numeric field to distribute
- **Detail (rows)** (grouping) — the row-level field. Enables per-row counting,
  cross-filtering and drill-down.
- **Tooltips** (measures, up to 10) — extra figures on hover, shown as averages over the bin

**Data volume**
The data reduction window is 30,000 rows; the visual requests the following segments
through `fetchMoreData` and combines them, so the distribution is computed over every row.
Verified at 500,000. **Power BI Desktop is the exception:** it runs inside Electron and
cannot stream segments, so it draws the first 30,000 and says so on the chart.

**Cross-filtering**
Clicking a bar applies a `BasicFilter` over the rows in the bin, which carries plain
scalars — the mechanism native slicers use for large value lists. It handles up to 10,000
distinct values in one click, measured as the practical ceiling over a live connection to
a semantic model. Above that the visual says so rather than filtering part of the bar:
filtering a subset would leave the rest of the report showing figures that do not match
what was clicked, and nothing on screen would reveal it.

**Privacy**
No telemetry, no analytics, no CDN, no endpoints of any kind. All calculation and
rendering happens inside Power BI, on your machine or your tenant. Nothing is stored
outside the report.

**Licensing**
Managed entirely through Microsoft AppSource with the official `IVisualLicenseManager`
API. No external account, no separate payment system, no licence server. Resolution is
asynchronous and never blocks rendering; a licence in its payment grace period keeps
working, and environments that cannot enforce licences — Publish to Web, embedded, PDF
export — never prompt a paying customer to buy what they already own.
