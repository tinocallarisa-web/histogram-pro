--- DESCRIPTION ---

Every other visual on your page tells you how much. A histogram tells you how it is spread — where the mass sits, how far the tail runs, and whether there are two populations hiding inside what looks like one.

Power BI has no native histogram. The usual workaround is a calculated column of bins in DAX: it fixes the bin count at design time and gives you no mean, no median and no quartiles. Histogram Pro is two field wells and no formulas.

WHAT YOU SEE IN THIS VIDEO
• A normal distribution: mean, median, quartiles, the normal curve and the cumulative line
• Small multiples: one histogram per plant, on the same bins and the same scale
• Value zones on sales data: "Over 13K: 20% of rows, 77% of value"
• A long-tailed measure: the clipped axis, and why the normal curve goes flat
• Click, Ctrl+click and a bookmark that brings the selection back

NEW IN 1.4
• Value zones (Pro): two cuts, three zones, each with its share of rows and of total value
• Small multiples (Pro): shared bins and scale, statistics per panel
• Cumulative frequency line (Pro) on its own 0–100% axis
• Bars coloured by the benchmark
• Visible selection, Ctrl+click and bookmarks
• Working drill-down and conditional formatting (fx) on bar colour
• Number formats from your model; English and Spanish

WHAT IT DOES
• The distribution over your whole table, not a sample — verified at 500,000 rows
• An axis built for skewed data: it focuses on where the data lives, and every row is still counted
• Mean, median, quartiles and IQR shading, a benchmark line, IBCS mode
• One click filters the report by every row behind the bar
• Keyboard navigation, screen reader labels, high contrast
• No network calls of any kind

FREE AND PRO
Free is the histogram itself, over your whole dataset, with no watermark: ten bins, mean and median, quartiles, benchmark, cross-filtering and bookmarks. Pro adds the bin count (2–100), outlier trimming, value zones, small multiples, the cumulative line, the normal curve, the statistics panel, value labels and bar styling. Turn on a Pro feature while editing and you see it working on your own data before deciding.
